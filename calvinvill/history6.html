The history of the Kouz Islands and often is representative of broader regional and global historical contexts of the Mediterranean region, North Africa, and Southern Europe. Geographically, the Kouz islands are primary composed of modern-day nations of Calvinvill and Khouza and a plurality of smaller islands, some of which are disputed by both nations. Written proof of inhabitance of the islands stretches back to approximately 703 CE (5037 Khouzan Calendar (KC)), where groups of Berbers from the Mahgreb fled Islam and the Umayyad Caliphate. Many people who currently inhabit the region can trace varrying degrees of their lineage back to these migrant Berbers. Through various encounters with regional powers throughout the 11th century, the Islands began to become integrated into the regional economy. . From the 15th century onwards, Portuguese traders had sustained contact with the Berbers of the Islands. As the industrial revolution swept the world, capital owners began to reshape and mature the regions economy. World War I and II reorientated the regions involvement with Europe, emerging from previous isolationist policy. This had profound effects on the culture, demographics, religion, and politics of the Kouz Islands, helping shape the dynamics we know today.

Early History and Berber Period

The island now known as Calvinvill was first inhabited by Berber or Amazigh peoples. The Berbers, indigenous to North Africa, particularly modern-day Morocco, Algeria, and Libya, originally spread Westward from the Nile. Around 703 CE (5037 KC), as the Umayyad Caliphate and Islam moved Westward, small groups of Berbers fled into the Mediterranean Sea and discovered the Kouz Islands chain.


Population centers were on the two main islands, Calvinvill, then called Tasrifa, and Khouza. The settlements, initially composed of hundreds of people, were a refuge from Umayyad rule, other Berbers, and conversion to Islam. The Berbers of Kouz maintained their traditional cultural and religious practices partially a result of the relative geographic isolation of the islands.

In the following centuries, these intial settlements expanded and a clear distinction developed between the Berbers of the Tasrifan Hinterlands, the Tasrifan Coast, and the surrounding Islands. Secluded by the surrounded mountain ranges, the Berbers of the Hinterlands were the most sedentary. Since the Hinterland Berbers lived in the most fertile land, they raised producive agricultural fields and livestock such as sheep and goats. In turn, the Hinterland clans were increasingly intertwined and became more sedentary. Whereas, the Berbers of the Tasrifan Coast and Kouz Islands, did not have access to such fertile lands. The livelihood of these clans were dependent on fishing and trade with the low Hinterlands, creating an inherent power imbalance between the Hinterlands, Coastal, and Island clans.

Middle Age European Encounters

See also: Slavery in the Kouz Islands

As Kouz Berbers reconfigured power dynamics, the development of the trans-Saharan slave trade would completely change racial dynamics of the island and exacerbate the newly formed inter-Berber dynamics. The trans-Saharan slave trade revolved around the Arab (or in some cases Berber) transport of people, namely Black sub-Saharan Africans or in other circumstances, mid-eastern people, Persians, and in fewer circumstances, Europeans.

In 1147 (5481 KC), a Fatimid cargo vessel named named Lulu transported captive human cargo composed of 100 Portuguese captives, over 20 Persian captives, 11 Mizrahi Jews from the Levant, and a handful of Muslim Berbers. Likely due to poor navigation and weather conditions, the Lulu veered off course and ran ashore on what now is Iuam Island. Upon the wreck of the Lulu, the survivors were found by a clan of Coastal Berbers on Iuam colloquially known as the Isemali. Historians and archaeologists agree that it is likely the enslaved captives banded together in the immediate aftermath of the wreck. The Fatimid sailors and officers, fearing revenge, fled the wreck site. It is disputed, but many believe the Fatimid sailors fled via driftwood or raft, to later wash ashore on the Western Coast of Khouza, later founding Khouza City.

The Lulu captives then integrated into various clans while maintaining their respective cultural and religious traditions. In the subsequent decades, there is evidence of an emergence of more hybrid religious and cultural practices, forming the origins of Khouzan religion.

Secularization

In the early 2000s, there was intense debate about the direction of the country. After long-term reign by the Ahmed family and a growing economic downturn, a large opposition movement began to emerge. The movement, nicknamed the Oranges, named after the orange farmworkers in Central and Southwestern CV. The nickname, and movement, emerged from the 2005 Farmworkers Movement, in which farmworkers (originating with orange farmworkers) were striking in protest of the proposed Farmland Reformation Law. The law would have reorganized land allocation, proposed state-mandated crop selection, and imposed regulations on pesticides among other growing techniques. The large farmworkers strikes, particularly in Nueva Santo Domingo, were joined by an array of opposition protestors. The largest opposition faction being the secularists, led by Robert Chubbles.

The Orange (Ouarehnhjei in Calvinese) movement then ballooned into a national cause, seeking political change throughout the nation. Chubbles was the de facto leader of the movement, but as the movement's popularity grew, there was a emergence of a militant faction, named the Orange Armed Guard (OAG or OEJ). This group engaged in numerous acts of terrorism which included; vehicle bombings, road closusures, and doxxing campaigns. Whereas the OAG/OEJ believed that secularism and the people of the Kouz Islands would only be truly free once the monarchy fell. Ideologically, the OAG/OEJ were far-left libertarians inspired by figures and movements such as Nongmin Uprising in Feiguojia, Kyær Resistance in historical Lotharia, and the writings of Wøjib Raknow and Joao Borges-Bastos. While the militant actions of the OAG/OEJ legitimized the demands of the Orange Movement, Chubbles disavowed the OAG/OEJ and believed the best path to a secular state was through reform of the monarchical system and dethroning the Ahmed Family. At the time, the OEJ operated anonymously. Years later, it was uncovered among the leaders were; Artemis Broderick, Genevive Wilson, Natasha Ngongbe, and Igor Ratshinikov. Each of the four led regional divisions within the country and collaborated with one another to a create a cohesive structure and ideological direction for the OAG/OEJ.

On 30 ahpiar 6340 (04/13/2006) Suzerain Nikolai Ahmed and his family members initiated negotiations with Robert Chubbles and other independence moderates including Albert Canterelli, Andres Almeida, Lars Püder, and Ali Figeroa, who were all colloquially known as the Alliance. The meeting, which was held later that day, was rumored to be fruitful and "a step in the right direction." However, tensions between Alliance moderates and the OAG/OEJ hit a boiling point a few days later on 32 ahpiar 6340 (04/15/2006). On that day, Andres Almeida and the chief negotiator for the Ahmed family, Abdul Achraf, were shot and killed. While nobody claimed responsibility for the killings, Alliance moderates and their followers suspected that the OAG/OEJ Western Division, led by Ratshinikov, were responsible. A large portion of general public viewed the killings as a last ditch effort by Nikolai Ahmed and his family to retain power. The culprits of the killings were never found and are still shrouded in mystery, controversy, and conspiracy to this day.

In the aftermath of the killing of Almeida and Achraf, Nikolai Ahmed secretly inquired to Robert Chubbles about a deal that would give Ahmed reign over the Khouzan, Iuam, and Mamadou islands, and transferred power over the rest of the country to Chubbles. A deal between Chubbles and Ahmed was reached on 35 ahpiar 6340 (04/17/2006) that installed Chubbles as leader of Calvinvill and ceded reign over Khouza Island to Ahmed with plans for further negotiations regarding Iuam and Mamadou Islands. The deal was highly controversial as it involved Chubbles secretly communicating with Ahmed without the knowledge of other Alliance members, especially Canterelli, who was jockeying to become leader of the new democratized nation. Regardless, Alliance members and the majority of the public celebrated the end of Ahmed rule. In a later speech, Chubbles defended his actions, stating "I acted in the best interest of the future of the country...I did not want a long drawn out negotiation negotiation process. I had an opportunity to act and I did."

This agreement changed the human geography of the islands and ignited a mass movement of peoples between islands. Former supporters and continued supporters of the Ahmed family, many of them ethnically Isemali, followed them to Khouza. Whereas, many existing civilians of Khouza fled to Sagitta and other places across the country. On Iuam and Mamadou Island, many people stayed, unsure of their status.

Left-wing farmworker protestors and the OAG/OEJ were furious about the Chubbles-Ahmed deal. These factions largely viewed the deal as appeasement to Ahmed's religious rule, a liberal power grab, and a attempt to thwart the growing revolutionary left.

OAG/OEJ Insurgency

In the aftermath of the Ahmed-Chubbles deal, Robert Chubbles was the de facto leader of the country. During this time, he began to lay the groundwork for the bureaucratic political and legislative system. Various public hearings and conventions with influential figures led to the creation of the Calvinvillian Constitution. The first constitution was published and distributed on 6 tsipeti 6341 (8/13/2007).

Following the Ahmed deal and the announcement of a constitution, the OAG/OEJ was publically dormant but secretly active. Full command of the OAG/OEG was assumed by Ratshinikov sometime during this period. Among Ratshinikovs' directives was the implementation of formalized military training. Ratshinikov had prior combat experience due to mandatory conscription in Zhestikray and fought for various guerrila insurgency groups in the Arsenic Republics in the Steppe. As a result, the Ratshinikov began to raise a very technically skilled fighting force compared to the prior amateur training. Rumors alledge that Ratshinikov harbored indirect support from the governments of Zhestikray and Feiguojia due to his prior paramilitary activity and the ideological vision of the OAG/OEJ.

Nearly three weeks after the Constitution was announced and ratified, on 25 tsipeti 6341, a vehicle bomb exploded in Downtown Calvin City. The incident killed two and injured fifteen. Days later, on 27 tsipeti, another bomb exploded near the Calvinvill Museum of Art that injured seventeen. Both incidents were claimed by OAG/OEJ leadership and were deemed "an announcement of war." Chubbles and the new Calvinvill government faced an imminent threat, as Chubbles did not foresee a military conflict in the aftermath of the Ahmed deal. Furthermore, after the deal was announced much of the Monarchical Guard, the previous fighting force under Ahmed rule, followed the family to Khouza. This left the new Calvinvill state with a weak fighting force without an existing structure. Scholars argue that the OAG/OEJ hiatus from the end of the war till the Calvin City Bombings was more importantly to signify to Chubbles that the left-wing faction of the protest movement had approved of the deal.

However, in the wake of the bombings, Chubbles immediately recognized his blunder, appointing Dugahbi Bearehei as the commander of the newly created Calvinvill Republican Guard Corps.
