Alphabet

The Calvinese alphabet is based on the Latin alphabet and consists of 26 letters. Each letter in the Calvinese alphabet is based on the phoentic pronunciation of the Latin alphabet. This means each 'letter' in the Calvinese alphabet often consists of a grapheme of two multiple Latin letters. Below is the standardized Calvinese alphabet, as there have been different past iterations.

Grammar

Since the Calvinese language acts like an alphabetical code, English grammar remains largely the same when translating. However there are some key differences. When a single letter repeats within a word a single apostrophe replaces the following letter.

For example:

Test --> ti'tsti; instead of tiitsti

When a 'chunk' repeats (like the Calvinese letter E) the repeating chunk is replaced with a "

For example:

Book --> beou"kh; instead of beououkh

Yet, with words that end in the same letter (like see or tree), what apostrophe to use can be ambiguous. However, the " should be used since the chunk is repeating.

For example:

See --> tsi"; instead of tsii

Calvinese punctuation mirrors English punctuation. However, since a singular apostrophe and quotation marks are used as grammar indicators in Calvinese, French guillemets are used when quoting someone or something. Since each letter in the Calvinese alphabet consists of two English letters, abbreviations are translated into Calvinese using the first letter of the corresponding Calvinese letter.

Translator

Calvinese is typically written in all lowercase, so despite the input, the output will be lowecase. The translator will immediately convert the quotes to guillemets. All other English punctuation will be preserved. Most importantly, do not use contractions, translations will be incorrect.

In addition to the romanized Calvinese alphabet, there was also the development of a script. The Calvinese script is composed of 21 individual graphemes, with three standalone graphemes (a e i). The other eighteen graphemes will combine into different pairs which will create 26 respective glyphs.

Metahistory

Calvinese first emerged as a concept around 2016, maybe a few years earlier. I think the desire for an alternative language emerged because of a desire to separate (both thematically and geographically) Calvinvill's history outside the direct sphere of the United States. I wanted Calvinvill to be different. To understand this vision I had, we need to understand Calvin in 2016. I had just spent six months in Arizona at that point, turning 14 that January. I started high school that August. My top three songs on Spotify were a british R&B song (Walk by Kwabs), On verra by Nekfeu, and La Marseillaise (the French national anthem). Lastly, Trump had been elected the preceding November and became president in January. This context is integral to understanding the initial creation of Calvinese: I was lonely, I was deeply ashamed to be an American (and started to be introduced to more radical politics), and lastly, I started learning French. Beginning to learn French opened up a different world. By looking at France I think I saw a nation legitimized by its language. However flawed that notion is, I believe it installed the idea that for in order for Calvinvill to be 'legitimate' (or as legitimate as a fake country can be) there needed to be a native language spoken there.

The script was developed recently, in July 2026. The creation of a script was more practical: I wanted a system that produced shorter words and looked better for heraldry.
